I am writing to apply for the \textit{PhD Studentship in Algorithms/Hardware Co-Design for Bayesian and Ising Models} (Reference: R143025) at Northeastern University London in collaboration with IMEC.

I hold a Bachelor of Technology in Computer Science with a specialisation in Artificial Intelligence from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham. I currently work as a Research Assistant at the SustainAI Lab at the Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati, investigating spiking state space models and their practical applications. My research in hardware-aware machine learning and physical neuromorphic computing  led me to explore how the properties of a computational system can be incorporated into algorithm design to improve computational efficiency. The opportunity to further explore this, is what led me to this project.

My work on \textit{SlimEdge} has given me direct experience in designing algorithms around hardware constraints. I developed a framework for optimising distributed neural network inference across heterogeneous edge devices, combining model pruning, performance estimation, and multi-objective optimisation to identify suitable deployments under latency, accuracy, memory, and device availability constraints. Evaluating thousands of deployment configurations on OPV2V and MVCNNs showed me how hardware characteristics shape various trade offs. \textit{SlimEdge} successfully optimized 96.88\% of all simulated scenarios under various device failures and memory constraints. This experience is directly relevant to the project’s objective of jointly optimising probabilistic algorithms and hardware architectures. I would bring experience in formulating constrained optimisation problems, evaluating competing objectives, and designing systematic experiments to assess computational trade offs.

My research on neuromorphic computing has approached the same challenge from the perspective of physical computation. I developed a reservoir model based on nonlinear oscillator dynamics and studied how its operating conditions affected temporal prediction performance. This research resulted in a paper, \textit{"Neuromorphic Computing Based on Parametrically Driven Oscillators and Frequency Combs"} that was accepted into \textit{Applied Physics Letters}. This work taught me to consider physical behaviour not merely as an implementation constraint, but as a potential source of computational capability. It showed that the parametric resonance regime of a 2:1 parametric resonance system provided better computational performance compared to the frequency comb regime. I see a compelling connection with the proposed research on stochastic MRAM devices. Although the underlying physical mechanisms differ, both research directions investigate how the properties of a physical system can be exploited for computation. My experience studying the relationship between physical behaviour and computational performance provides a relevant foundation for this work.

What attracts me to this studentship is the challenge of translating device level stochasticity into useful algorithmic behaviour. In probabilistic computing, the statistical properties of physical devices influence how reliably and efficiently Bayesian inference and Ising model optimisation can be performed. Rather than treating device variability as noise to be eliminated, I would like to explore how it can be exploited through algorithms designed around the underlying hardware. The project's combination of algorithm development, physical device behaviour, and hardware-software co-design aligns closely with the questions that have motivated my research to date.

I would welcome the opportunity to contribute to the collaborative research at Northeastern University London, University of Kent, and IMEC. The project would also allow me to deepen my expertise in probabilistic modelling and emerging memory technologies. My experience in hardware constrained optimisation and physical computing provides a strong foundation for this work. I see this studentship as an opportunity to build on that foundation while contributing to research that combines probabilistic algorithms with emerging memory technologies.

Thank you for considering my application.
