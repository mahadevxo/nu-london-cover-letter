I am writing to apply for the \textit{PhD Studentship in Algorithms/Hardware Co-Design for Bayesian and Ising Models} (Reference: R143025) at Northeastern University London in collaboration with IMEC.

I graduated from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham with a Bachelor of Technology in Computer Science with a specialisation in Artificial Intelligence. Currently, I work as a Research Assistant at the SustainAI Lab at the Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati where I work on  spiking state space models and their applications. My research on physical computing and hardware-aware machine learning led me to the study of how the features of a computational system can be used in developing algorithms for better computational efficiency. The opportunity to further explore this, is what led me to this project.

My work on \textit{SlimEdge} gave me hands-on experience in designing algorithms in accordance with the properties of hardware. It lead to the  development of a framework for the optimisation of the inference of distributed neural networks across heterogeneous edge devices. It integrated model pruning, performance estimation, and multi-objective optimisation to find suitable deployments in terms of latency, accuracy, memory, and device availability constraints. Evaluating thousands of deployment configurations for OPV2V and MVCNNs showed me the role played by the hardware in various trade-offs. \textit{SlimEdge} managed to optimise 96.88\% of all simulated scenarios in the under various device failures and memory constraints. This experience is directly relevant to the proposed project’s objective of jointly optimising probabilistic algorithms and hardware architectures. I would bring experience in problem formulation, evaluation of competing objectives, and conducting experiments to assess computational trade-offs.

In my research on neuromorphic computing, the same challenge was approached from the perspective of physical computation. I developed a reservoir model based on nonlinear oscillator dynamics and researched the effect of its operating conditions on temporal prediction performance. This research resulted in a publication, \textit{``Neuromorphic Computing Based on Parametrically Driven Oscillators and Frequency Combs''} that was accepted in \textit{Applied Physics Letters}. This research experience made me consider that physical properties not just as a way of implementing algorithms, but as a potential source of computational capability. The research found that the parametric resonance regime of a 2:1 parametric resonance system provides better computational performance compared to the frequency comb regime. I see a strong connection with the proposed research on stochastic MRAM devices. Although the underlying physical mechanisms differ, both research directions investigate how the properties of a physical system can be exploited for computation. My experience studying the relationship between physical behaviour and computational performance provides a relevant foundation for this work.

This studentship interests me as it addresses the challenge of mapping device-level stochasticity to algorithmic behaviour. In probabilistic computing, the statistical properties of physical devices affect the reliability and efficiency of performing Bayesian inference and Ising model optimisation. I would like to use this property through algorithms that take into account the underlying hardware instead of eliminating the variance of the devices. The combination of algorithm development, physical device behaviour and hardware-software co-design in this project will directly connect to the questions that I have been researching to date.

I would be delighted to have the opportunity to contribute to the collaborative research at Northeastern University London, University of Kent, and IMEC. The project would also allow me to deepen my expertise in probabilistic modelling and emerging memory technologies. My experience in hardware constrained optimisation and physical computing provides a strong foundation for this work. I see this studentship as an opportunity to build on that foundation while contributing to research that combines probabilistic algorithms with emerging memory technologies.
